The serotonin story let us treat depression as a missing nutrient. It was kind in the short run. It was wrong in a way that left a lot of people stranded when the nutrient didn't show. The predictive-brain story is harder, because it asks the body and the schedule and the inflammation and the past all to count as part of the diagnosis. It's also more hopeful in a particular way — forecasts can be retrained, and the brain is built to do exactly that whenever the evidence reaches it. So if you're in this somewhere, the question isn't what's broken in my chemistry. The question is what does my brain currently expect, and what is the smallest piece of evidence I can hand it today that contradicts that expectation. Not an affirmation. Evidence. A walk. A call. A meal. A scheduled hour of light. The model updates from the bottom. It always has.
