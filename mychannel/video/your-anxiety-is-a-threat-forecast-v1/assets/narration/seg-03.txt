Here is the strange part. The forecast does not stay in your head. It recruits the body until the prediction starts to feel like proof. Barrett and Simmons argued in a 2015 Nature Reviews Neuroscience paper that the brain constantly makes interoceptive predictions, guesses about the body's internal state, then uses incoming signals to update or confirm them. You do not simply notice your heartbeat, breath, stomach, heat, and tension. Your brain is predicting what those signals mean before they fully arrive. So anxiety can become circular in a very intimate way. Paulus and Stein, in a 2006 Biological Psychiatry paper, described anxiety through an insular and interoceptive lens, where anxious brains may amplify the gap between predicted and actual bodily states and interpret ordinary body noise as threat. A flutter becomes evidence. A skipped breath becomes evidence. Heat in the face becomes evidence. Then the evidence intensifies the forecast, and the forecast intensifies the body. You are not lying to yourself. You are inhabiting a prediction that has learned how to manufacture its own supporting data. This is why the phrase just calm down is so useless. Calm down from the body that already feels endangered, and from the mind that sees that body and says there must be a reason. Sensation becomes interpretation, interpretation becomes more sensation, and the loop begins to feel like perception rather than prediction. There is a quiet mercy in seeing this clearly. The racing heart is not necessarily proof that the meeting is unsafe. The nausea is not necessarily proof that the relationship is wrong. The electric restlessness is not necessarily proof that your life is about to collapse. It may be your brain preparing a body for a future it has not verified. The forecast made flesh. And once you understand that, the next question is why the forecast keeps surviving even after the catastrophe never comes.
